\begin{abstract}

\begin{revision}
Quantum implementations of Shor's order-finding on elliptic curves have established the feasibility of coherent group arithmetic and provided detailed resource estimates. Building on these results, this work presents a modular hybrid architecture that combines quantum order finding for the periodic function $f_P(k)=kP$, classical recovery and validation of the point order, and integration withn an ECDSA-like signing workflow. The quantum stage follows the standard order-finding procedure and evaluates the periodic function through reversible elliptic-curve arithmetic based on established constructions. The contribution is twofold: and explicit integration of these components into a classical--quantum process, and a computational cost analysis that distinghuishes controlled point addition from full scalar multiplication. The comparison with classical methods separates direct enumeration, generic group algorithms, and approaches based on point counting and factorization of the group order, making the assumptions and scope of the comparison explicit. A worked example over a small prime field provides a numerical validation of period recovery and its use within the classical signing workflow.

\end{revision}

\textbf{Keywords:} Quantum computing, Shor's algorithm, ECDSA, secp256k1, hybrid algorithms, blockchain cryptography, order finding.

\end{abstract}
